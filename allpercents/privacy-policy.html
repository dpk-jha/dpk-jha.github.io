AllPercents — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 6, 2026

No name, email, location or ads

Overview

AllPercents (com.dpkay.apps.allpercents) shows the battery level of your own devices — phone, tablet, watch, laptop — on each other, and warns you before one of them runs out. Each device sends its battery level to a small server that runs on Cloudflare, and the server passes it on to your other devices and works out when an alert is due.

This policy describes how Deepak Jha (“we”, “our”, or “us”) handles information in connection with the app on Android, iPhone, Apple Watch, Mac, Windows and Wear OS (the “App”). Unlike a fully offline app, AllPercents does send your battery data to our server — that is how it works. We do not ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts or location, and the App contains no advertising and no analytics.

There is no sign-up form. The first device creates an account silently; you add further devices with a short code shown on one device and typed on another, or approve a new device from a code it shows. An optional recovery key lets you get back in if you lose every device.

What We Store on Our Server

The server can read the following. It is kept only while your account exists, unless a shorter time is given:

Your devices: for each one its name, model, platform (for example “Galaxy A52, Android”), icon and colour, its place in the list, whether it appears in widgets, and when it was added. The names are whatever you or the device chose; they may contain a real name if you type one.
Battery state: the latest reading of each device — percent, power source, charging state, charge rate where the device reports it, and when it was sent. Readings from the last two hours are also kept, to tell how fast a battery is draining.
Your settings for alerts, quiet hours, widgets and devices.
Alerts: the last 500 alerts sent for the account, with their text (which contains the device name and the percent).
Bluetooth accessories you choose to share (for example earbuds): name, icon, battery percent, whether connected, and when last seen. Nothing is shared unless you turn it on for that accessory.
An account id — a random identifier, not linked to your name or email.

We do not hide this data from ourselves: the server reads battery levels and device names so that it can send alerts without your phone staying awake. What the server cannot read is described next.

What We Cannot Read

Your device’s secret. Each device holds a secret that proves it belongs to your account. The server keeps only a one-way hash of it, so a copy of our storage cannot be used to act as your device.
Your recovery key, if you make one. It is shown once; the server keeps only a keyed hash. Make a new one and the old one stops working.
Pairing codes. Codes that add a device, and requests from a device waiting to be approved, are kept as hashes for ten minutes and then forgotten.
Push identifiers (Android notifications, see below). They are stored encrypted and tied to the device they belong to.

What We Do Not Collect

Your name, email address, phone number or postal address
Your location — the App never asks for it
Contacts, call logs, messages, photos, files or the content of other apps
Advertising IDs, or anything used to track you across apps or websites
Usage analytics or crash reports (the App has no analytics or crash-reporting library)
Which apps you use, what you do on your device, or Bluetooth device addresses (the address of an accessory stays on the phone that reads it)

Network Addresses

Every request reaches our server through Cloudflare, which processes network (IP) addresses as our hosting provider, under its own policy. To limit abuse, our server counts requests per address for one hour, and keeps only a one-way hash of the address for that counter. We do not store addresses with your account, and stored request logs are switched off.

Permissions (Android)

INTERNET
Sends this device’s battery level to the server and receives your other devices’ levels and alerts. The server only accepts encrypted (HTTPS) connections.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS
Shows the alerts you ask for, such as “Galaxy A52 is at 15%”. You can decline; the App then shows levels but cannot warn you.
BLUETOOTH · BLUETOOTH_CONNECT
Lets the phone read the battery level of Bluetooth accessories you pick and, if you choose, share it with your other devices. The App does not scan for or store the addresses of unrelated devices.
REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS
Lets the App ask you to exempt it from Android battery optimisation so that reports and alerts are not delayed. It is a request you can refuse; nothing is collected by it.

Third-Party Services

The App has no analytics libraries, no advertising networks and no crash-reporting tools. Information goes to these services only because the App needs them:

Cloudflare
Hosts the server described above (Workers and Durable Objects). Everything under “What We Store” is stored there. Cloudflare’s privacy policy.
Google Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android only)
Delivers a short wake-up message when an alert is due, so your phone does not have to keep asking. The message carries no battery level, device name or alert text — it only tells the App that something new is waiting, and the App then asks our server for it. To send it, Google receives a Firebase installation ID for the App on that phone. Google’s privacy policy.

We do not sell or rent data, and we do not share it with anyone else.

On Your Device

The secret that identifies a device is kept in the platform’s protected storage: the Android Keystore, the iOS Keychain, Windows data protection (DPAPI). On a Mac it is kept in a file readable only by your user account. The App also keeps the last list of devices and alerts it received so it can show them offline. Removing the App, or signing the device out, deletes these.

Your Choices and Deleting Your Data

Everything below is in the App’s Settings, on any of your devices:

  • Remove a device — it is signed out at once, and its readings, settings, shared accessories and push identifier are erased from the server. Alerts already sent stay in the list of recent alerts until newer ones replace them.
  • Sign out all other devices — if one is lost.
  • Delete the account — erases your devices, readings, settings, alerts, accessories and recovery key from the server, and every device is signed out. This cannot be undone.
  • Stop sharing an accessory, or turn off notifications in the system settings.

Because there is no email or name, we cannot look up an account from a request: use the App. If you can no longer open it on any device, use your recovery key; without either, we cannot tell which account is yours.

Children's Privacy

The App is not directed at children under the age of 13. We do not knowingly collect any information from children.
